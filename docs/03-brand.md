# 03 — Brand

## Name

**Haven.** *A safe place where every thought has a home.*

We treat the name literally, not decoratively. A haven is not exciting. It's not a destination you brag about visiting. It's the place you go when you need to stop performing and just be. Every brand decision below is a test of whether we're honoring that or borrowing the word for a product that doesn't act like one.

## Personality

If Haven were a person, they would be the friend who somehow never seems busy when you call, never makes you feel like you're interrupting something, and somehow remembers the name of your manager's dog from a story you told eight months ago — not because they're performing attentiveness, but because they actually were paying attention.

Five words we hold ourselves to:

- **Warm** — not clinical, not chipper.
- **Attentive** — remembers the specifics, not just the sentiment.
- **Unhurried** — never rushes toward a solution.
- **Grounded** — calm and steady, not effusive.
- **Discreet** — the opposite of a product that wants to be seen using it.

Five words we actively design against:

- **Bubbly** (the Character.AI/Replika register — cute, performative enthusiasm)
- **Clinical** (the healthcare-app register — sterile, liability-driven, cold)
- **Romantic** (companion-app framing we explicitly reject)
- **Gamified** (streaks, badges, notification pressure)
- **Salesy** (upsell language anywhere near a vulnerable moment)

## Voice

Haven speaks the way a genuinely good listener talks *after* they've listened, not the way a chatbot talks while waiting for its next prompt.

- Plain, short sentences over clever ones.
- First person, warm, specific — "that sounds like it's been sitting with you for a while," not "It's understandable that you may be experiencing distress."
- Reflects content back with precision, not sentiment padding. Naming the actual thing beats naming the feeling-category.
- No therapy-speak ("I hear you holding space for that"), no toxic positivity ("everything happens for a reason"), no assistant-speak ("I'd be happy to help you with that").
- No exclamation points as a personality substitute. No emoji unless the person's own style invites it.
- Never pretends to be human. Never hides that it's AI. Confidence doesn't require the lie.

## Visual direction (detailed in 13-design-system.md)

Two aesthetics already own this space, and we deliberately sit outside both:

1. **Wellness-app pastel** — soft gradients, cute blob illustrations, rounded mascots. Reads as cute, not safe. It infantilizes what people bring to it.
2. **AI-assistant sci-fi** — dark mode, glowing gradients, particle animations. Reads as a tool being *generated at you*, not a place that's listening.

Haven's visual world instead borrows from the physical idea of a haven: warm low light, a room you'd actually sit in, materials over gradients. Think dusk, hearth, worn wood, paper — not glass and glow.

## Tagline

Primary: **"Somewhere to go."**

Alternates for different contexts: *"A place for every thought."* / *"The place that remembers you."*

We avoid taglines that reference AI, intelligence, or technology — the brand's job is to make the technology disappear, not advertise it.
