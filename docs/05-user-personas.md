# 05 — User Personas

We deliberately did not build personas around demographics (age, income, job title) as the primary axis. Two people with identical demographics can have completely different relationships to being heard. Our personas are built around **emotional access patterns** — how much a person's real feelings currently get expressed to anyone, and why.

## Primary: The Unheard Achiever

**Who they are:** Late 20s to early 40s. Outwardly doing well — good job, stable enough life, people would describe them as "put together." Mai is a product manager. She's good at her job, well-liked, and hasn't cried in front of another person in over a year, not because nothing's wrong but because she's the one people come to, not the one who gets to fall apart.

**What's actually going on:** She has real things weighing on her — a relationship that's quietly deteriorating, ambition she's starting to doubt, a low hum of loneliness she can't name to anyone because on paper her life looks fine, and saying "I'm not okay" out loud feels like it would surprise people in a way she'd have to manage.

**Why existing options fail her:** Friends are busy and she doesn't want to be the friend with a problem every time they talk. Therapy is on her list but booking the first appointment has been on her list for eight months. Journaling lasted eleven days before it started to feel like writing into a void. She's used ChatGPT to vent once, late at night, and it gave her a bulleted list of coping strategies. She closed the tab.

**What she needs from Haven:** To say the actual, specific, un-impressive thing that happened — not a curated version — and have it land. Not be fixed. Not be redirected to breathing exercises. Just landed.

## Secondary: The Late-Night Overthinker

**Who they are:** Anxious ruminator, any age, any life stage. Duc is 24, still lives with his parents, and does his real thinking between 1 and 3 a.m., when the thoughts he's been holding all day finally get loud enough that he can't ignore them and everyone he'd normally text is asleep.

**What's actually going on:** The thought spiral itself isn't the whole problem — it's that he has nowhere to put it *at 1 a.m.*, so it just loops, unwitnessed, until he's exhausted enough to sleep or not.

**Why existing options fail him:** Nobody is awake. A journal doesn't talk back, which at 1 a.m. makes the loop worse, not better — writing it down without a response can feel like confirming no one's there. ChatGPT is available but it answers like a search engine that happens to use "I" — competent, generic, forgets by morning.

**What Haven needs to do for him:** Be there at 1 a.m. without it being weird that it's 1 a.m. Not diagnose the anxiety. Just take the specific loop seriously enough to interrupt it with actual attention.

## Tertiary: The Quietly Grieving

**Who they are:** Going through a loss, breakup, or life transition — recent enough that it's still the main thing, old enough that everyone around them has moved on to asking about other things. Elena's mother died four months ago. People stopped asking "how are you holding up" around month two.

**What's actually going on:** She doesn't need new sympathy — she's had plenty. She needs to keep talking about her mother, in detail, on the days that detail resurfaces, without watching people's faces do the "we've already done this" flicker.

**Why existing options fail her:** Friends and family grieved too and have their own healing timeline; repeating the same story to them starts to feel like a burden. A therapist would help but feels like a big commitment for "I just want to tell someone that Mom used to hum a specific song."

**What Haven needs to do for her:** Never get tired of the story. Actually remember the details from the last time she told it, so it never feels like starting over.

## Who Haven is explicitly not for (at least not first)

- Someone in active crisis who needs immediate human intervention — Haven's job here is detection and handoff, not engagement (see [11-product-prd.md](11-product-prd.md)).
- Someone seeking romantic or sexual role-play from an AI — a different, much larger, and much more ethically fraught product we are deliberately not building.
- Someone who wants an AI to make decisions or give directive life advice — Haven reflects; it does not decide for people.
