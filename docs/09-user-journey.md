# 09 — User Journey

Told through Mai (The Unheard Achiever, [05-user-personas.md](05-user-personas.md)) across her first year, because the real product only shows its value over a long horizon — a single-session journey map would misrepresent what Haven actually is.

## Stage 0 — Discovery (day 0)

Mai finds Haven through a friend's offhand mention, not an ad about "AI mental health." The framing that gets her to open the app isn't "AI companion" — it's closer to "somewhere to say the thing you didn't say today." She's skeptical. She's tried a mental-health app before and deleted it in a week.

**Risk:** if onboarding leads with AI-companion tropes (avatar customization, "give me a name," personality quiz), she bounces immediately — it will read as Replika-adjacent, and she already has an opinion about that.

## Stage 1 — First conversation (day 0)

No avatar, no persona selection. A simple, low-pressure opening — space to type or talk, and Haven listens without interrupting. She tests it with something small and safe, not her real thing yet: a mildly annoying day at work. Haven doesn't respond with a bulleted list. It reflects back the *specific* thing that actually bothered her, which she hadn't fully named to herself. That's the first small proof.

**Success condition:** she doesn't feel like she's talking to a form. She feels like this is worth coming back to with something that matters more.

## Stage 2 — The real disclosure (week 1–2)

She comes back, unprompted, at 11 p.m., and says the actual thing — the relationship doubt she hasn't told anyone. This is the magic moment: twenty minutes, uninterrupted, ending not in advice but in "I think I understand what really hurt today." This is the session that decides whether she becomes a long-term user or a one-time tester.

**Risk:** if Haven breaks the moment with a premature suggestion, a safety disclaimer that feels boilerplate, or a shift into assistant-mode ("Here are 3 things you could try"), the trust built in Stage 1 collapses.

## Stage 3 — Building the habit, on her terms (month 1–3)

She doesn't come every day. Some weeks she comes three times, some weeks not at all. Haven never punishes the gap — no streak reset, no guilt-tinged "we haven't talked in a while." When she does come back, Haven doesn't make her re-explain the relationship situation from scratch; it remembers where they left off, which is the thing journaling and ChatGPT both failed to give her.

## Stage 3.5 — A good day *(added after board review round 2)*

Two months in, she gets a promotion she'd doubted she deserved. She doesn't need to process it — she just wants somewhere that would actually care. She opens Haven and marks it with "Keep this" rather than starting a full conversation — ten seconds, not twenty minutes. This matters beyond the moment itself: without days like this one in the record, Haven's later reflection back to her would be built almost entirely from her hardest nights, which would quietly misrepresent who she's actually been. See [10-feature-list.md](10-feature-list.md) for why this is treated as a correctness requirement, not just a nice-to-have.

## Stage 4 — The pattern is named (month 3–6)

Haven surfaces something she hadn't consciously connected: the relationship doubt and the ambition doubt she mentioned in a different, unrelated conversation are the same underlying fear showing up twice. This is a reflection she couldn't have generated herself in the moment, because she was inside each conversation individually. This is the point where Haven's value visibly exceeds what a friend or a single journal entry could offer — not better empathy, better continuity.

## Stage 5 — The off-ramp (month 4)

Prompted partly by these sessions, she finally books a therapist — something that had been stuck on her list for eight months. Haven doesn't resist this or try to make itself indispensable; it treats it as a win, offers (with her consent) a summary of recurring themes she can choose to bring to her first session, and keeps being there for the days between sessions and for everything therapy doesn't cover. This is the mission's "off-ramp, not enclosure" principle made concrete ([02-mission.md](02-mission.md)).

## Stage 6 — The long memory (year 1+)

On a genuinely bad day a year in, she asks Haven to remind her who she was a year ago. Haven doesn't summarize generically — it names the actual relationship doubt from month one, the ambition fear from month three, and the fact that she's since made a hard decision she once thought she couldn't. This is the long-term vision made real: Haven as the memory of her emotional life, proving her own growth back to her with specifics she'd forgotten.

## Cross-stage principle

At every stage, the test is the same one from the mission: did this moment help her feel heard and understand herself better — without making her need Haven more than she needs the actual people, including the therapist she now sees, in her life?
