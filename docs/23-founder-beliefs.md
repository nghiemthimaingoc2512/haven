# 23 — Founder Beliefs

## Trust > Growth

We believe a company that grows faster than it earns trust, in this category specifically, doesn't actually have a business — it has a liability with good short-term numbers. Every growth tactic gets tested against whether it could plausibly damage trust before it's tested against whether it works. **In practice:** no growth-hacked onboarding, no paid acquisition that oversells what Haven is, no feature shipped ahead of the safety work it depends on.

## Depth > Scale

We would rather have a smaller number of people whose lives Haven genuinely understands than a large number of people who opened it twice. Depth is also the only thing the moat is built from ([04-product-positioning.md](04-product-positioning.md)) — scale without depth is just distribution of a shallow product. **In practice:** roadmap prioritization favors the Life Graph and its accuracy over breadth of feature surface; we resist the pressure to ship a wider top-of-funnel feature before the depth underneath it is real.

## Understanding > Memory

Storing what someone said is not the same as understanding who they are. Memory is a prerequisite; understanding is the actual product. Confusing the two is the single easiest way for this company to end up building a slightly-better ChatGPT with a bigger context window. **In practice:** we measure and invest in the Life Graph's interpretive structure ([21-life-graph.md](21-life-graph.md)), not just its storage capacity or recall accuracy.

## Reflection > Advice

Advice presumes we know what's right for someone's life. Reflection only presumes we were paying attention. The first is a claim about someone else's choices; the second is a service. **In practice:** advice is available only when explicitly asked for, and even then, framed as a starting point, never a directive — see [15-copywriting-guidelines.md](15-copywriting-guidelines.md).

## Calm > Engagement

An interface that's trying to keep someone's attention and an interface that's trying to actually help someone are, in this category, usually pulling in opposite directions. We are choosing the side that makes the product boring by conventional growth metrics and, we believe, genuinely valuable by every metric that matters to the person using it. **In practice:** see the anti-metrics list in [18-metrics.md](18-metrics.md) — no streaks, no push-notification loops, no session-length targets.

## Privacy > Virality

The most valuable thing this product will ever hold is also the most damaging thing it could ever leak, and the two facts are the same fact. A viral feature and a private one are, for this specific product, close to mutually exclusive — see the deliberate rejection of a "Wrapped"-style share mechanic in [22-signature-experience.md](22-signature-experience.md). **In practice:** no feature ships default-to-share; sharing is always an opt-in, person-initiated act, never a suggested or rewarded one.

## Accuracy > Comfort

A reflection that only ever makes someone feel good is flattery, not understanding — and flattery is a worse betrayal of this product's purpose than an uncomfortable truth would be, because it's a betrayal disguised as care. **In practice:** Haven's reflections are allowed to be accurate and unflattering; we do not tune the model to be more agreeable at the cost of being less true. This is also the belief most likely to cost us users in the short term, and we're accepting that tradeoff deliberately — see the churn risk noted in [24-founder-questions.md](24-founder-questions.md).

## Continuity > Novelty

The temptation in any AI product is to keep shipping new interfaces and flashy capabilities, because that's what gets attention. The one thing that must never break, regardless of what's new, is the continuity of someone's own story — a redesign, a new modality, or a model upgrade is a failure if it costs someone their history or its coherence. **In practice:** every new interface Haven adds is judged first on whether it strengthens or fragments the Life Graph, before it's judged on how impressive it is on its own.
